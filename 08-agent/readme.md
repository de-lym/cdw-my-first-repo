# The Dream Guide

# Project Ideas

## Concept

A conversational dream guide embedded in the Dream Archive. It helps visitors find words for a dream through short sensory questions rather than interpreting the dream for them.

## Object Direction

The guide works as a writing companion rather than an interpreter. Its purpose is to help a visitor describe a dream, move from vague recollection toward language, and optionally turn that description into an archive contribution.

# Digital Objects

## Digital Object

The central object is a constrained conversational persona. Its rules specify one question per reply, one to three short sentences, sensory prompts, no diagnosis or psychoanalysis, and no requests for identifying details.

The constraints are part of the object itself: the guide should feel focused and attentive rather than like a general-purpose chatbot.

## Interaction

The visitor can begin with a feeling, image, fragment, or single word. The guide responds with one short sensory question at a time, helping the visitor gradually develop a more detailed description of the dream.

The conversation should remain open-ended. The guide does not determine what the dream means, but creates space for the visitor to describe what they remember.

# Aesthetic

## Visual Language

The guide inherits the Dreamcore environment while living inside the warmer Build Your Dream composer. Its interface should remain quiet and intimate so the visitor’s own words stay central.

It should avoid the visual conventions of a productivity tool or conventional AI chatbot. The interface should feel like a space for reflection rather than a utility for completing a task.

### Typography

The guide uses the composer’s readable interface typography and compact message layout. Typography should prioritize conversational clarity while maintaining the visual relationship with the wider Dream Archive.

## Layout

The conversational surface remains compact and contained within the Dream Composer. The message area, input, send control, status feedback, and save action should feel like parts of one continuous interaction rather than a collection of separate interface controls.

## Motion

Conversation should unfold in short steps. Message appearance, loading states, and subtle interface responses provide enough motion to communicate that the guide is responding without turning the interaction into a chat-feed spectacle.

Motion should support the rhythm of reflection rather than create urgency.

## Atmosphere

The guide should feel exploratory, intimate, and slightly uncertain rather than instructional. Its visual identity comes from the relationship between quiet space, typography, interaction, and the act of remembering a dream.

## Less Explanation, More Encounter

The Dream Guide should help visitors describe their dreams without telling them what those dreams mean. Its role is to create prompts for reflection rather than provide conclusions.

## Hierarchy

The visitor’s language is the primary material of the object. The guide’s responses should remain shorter and visually secondary so that the conversation gradually becomes a record of the visitor’s own recollection.

## Preserve Ambiguity

Dreams do not need to be resolved into a single interpretation. The guide should preserve uncertainty, contradictions, incomplete memories, and sensory fragments as meaningful parts of the experience.

## Participation
