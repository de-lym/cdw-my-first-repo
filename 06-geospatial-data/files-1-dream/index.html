About this data

Each country is shaded by its average nightly time in bed, drawn from a 48-market sleep-tracking study compiled by World Population Review. Warm amber marks the least rest; cool violet marks the most — a gradient meant to echo the sky's own drift from dusk into deep night. Boundaries unmatched to the dataset sit quietly in the dark, unlit.

Where this could go next

A geospatial structure like this one could just as easily hold something more personal than population averages: a dream journal, geotagged to the real or half-remembered places each dream was set in. Recurring dream locations could cluster as heatmap density, and a timeline slider could let a viewer scrub through months of sleep to watch which places the mind keeps returning to — turning an individual, interior archive into something you can navigate the way you'd navigate a city.

Data: World Population Review, "Average Sleep Time by Country" (2026), based on ENTRAIN sleep-tracking research. Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:110m via nvkelso/natural-earth-vector.
