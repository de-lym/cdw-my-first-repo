# Dreamcore: Between Memory and Reality

# Project Ideas

## Concept

A dreamlike website that asks visitors to wander through an environment rather than follow a conventional linear story. It draws from Dreamcore, nostalgia, liminal spaces, surreal imagery, and the uncertainty of fragmented memory.

The experience should feel familiar but impossible at the same time, allowing visitors to move through something that resembles a forgotten memory rather than a traditional website.

## Object Direction

The website functions as a spatial archive in which information becomes a collection of movable fragments. Instead of following a predetermined sequence, visitors construct their own route through floating bubbles, popup windows, environmental details, and unexpected textual fragments.

The archive should create a sense of discovery without requiring visitors to understand everything immediately.

## Spatial Experience

A full-screen dream landscape contains a sky, drifting clouds, a translucent liminal pool, VHS-inspired overlays, grain, and eight floating letter or number bubbles.

Each bubble opens a movable browser-like window containing a different section or fragment of the archive. The homepage therefore remains the primary environment rather than becoming a conventional navigation page.

## Interaction

Visitors can hover and click the floating bubbles, open and close browser windows, move windows around the screen, and enter the pool to encounter an unpredictable textual fragment.

The interface should allow visitors to construct their own path through the archive rather than directing them through a fixed sequence.

# Digital Objects

## Digital Object

The central digital object is the Dreamcore environment itself: a full-screen landscape that contains floating navigation bubbles, movable popup windows, a liminal pool, and fragments of text that behave like recovered memories.

The individual windows function as artifacts within the larger environment rather than as separate conventional webpages.

## Spatial Composition

The main environment consists of:

- a full-screen animated sky
- drifting clouds
- a translucent liminal pool
- VHS scanlines and film grain
- eight floating bubble buttons
- movable browser-like popup windows
- an interactive “Enter the Pool” mode

The composition uses negative space deliberately so that each element feels isolated within a larger dream landscape.

## Motion

Clouds drift slowly across the background, the pool shimmers, bubbles float, grain moves continuously, and popup windows appear through gentle transitions.

Motion should feel like drifting through an environment rather than receiving conventional interface feedback.

## Interactive Fragments

The “Enter the Pool” interaction changes the atmosphere and reveals randomly selected textual fragments, such as memories, announcements, or impossible observations.

Randomness allows the experience to change from one interaction to another, reinforcing the feeling of fragmented and unreliable memory.

## Content Structure

Popup content is treated as a collection of fragments rather than a sequence of pages. Visitors can open several windows, move them across the environment, and construct temporary relationships between different pieces of information.

The archive therefore becomes something visitors arrange and interpret rather than something they simply read.

## Design Principles

- Describe the object as an experience, not as a list of implementation steps.
- Preserve the relationship between concept, visual form, and interaction.
- Keep technical mechanisms subordinate to the meaning of the object.
- Treat every popup, canvas, map, chart, network, or composer as a self-contained digital artifact.
- Maintain the archive's sense of discovery.

# Dreamcore: Between Memory and Reality

## Concept

A dreamlike website that asks visitors to wander through an environment rather than follow a conventional linear story. It draws from Dreamcore, nostalgia, liminal spaces, surreal imagery, and the uncertainty of fragmented memory.

## Visual Language

The visual language combines nostalgia, liminal spaces, soft dream imagery, and subtle digital artifacts.

The environment should balance comfort and unease: familiar colors and playful forms are softened by empty space, analog textures, ambiguity, and unexpected moments.

### Color Palette

The palette is soft and slightly faded, resembling old photographs and remembered dream imagery.

- Pale blue
- Soft lavender
- Warm cream
- Light pink
- Fog gray
- Muted yellow

Bright colors should appear sparingly as uncanny accents, similar to sudden vivid details within a dream.

### Typography

Typography should feel familiar and slightly playful rather than overtly futuristic.

- Bagel Fat One is used for the floating bubble letters, giving the navigation a childlike and dreamlike personality.

- Space Mono is used for popup titles and interface elements, creating a subtle connection to old computer systems.

- IBM Plex Mono is used for body text and archival information, reinforcing the feeling of reading fragments from a digital archive.

Large headings, small body text, lowercase labels, and generous spacing contribute to the atmosphere without making typography itself the primary visual effect.

## Layout

The homepage is intentionally minimal and spacious. Floating bubbles are distributed across the screen with generous negative space, creating the feeling of objects suspended within a larger environment.

Popup windows appear above the landscape and can be moved freely. Their placement should remain temporary and changeable rather than behaving like fixed webpage sections.

The layout should preserve the relationship between empty space and isolated visual fragments.

## Motion

Motion is subtle and continuous.

Clouds, bubbles, the pool, grain, and popup transitions should move slowly enough to create an ambient sense of time passing.

The movement should remain calm and slightly unpredictable, avoiding the speed and responsiveness associated with conventional application interfaces.

## Interaction

Navigation should feel almost invisible.

Hover states, popup transitions, draggable windows, and environmental changes should provide enough feedback without introducing a large collection of buttons or controls.

The visitor should feel as though they are moving through a place rather than operating an interface.


## Atmosphere

The emotional tone combines:

- nostalgia
- curiosity
- loneliness
- comfort
- wonder
- uncertainty

The website should not provide a single interpretation of the dream environment. Instead, it should leave enough ambiguity for visitors to construct their own associations and meanings.

## Website Components

### HTML

The HTML establishes the Dreamcore landscape and popup system as a single interactive environment.

The structure includes the full-screen background, cloud and pool layers, VHS and grain overlays, floating navigation bubbles, and the popup layer used to display archive content.

The homepage should remain an interactive dream landscape rather than becoming a collection of conventional navigation sections.

### CSS

The CSS creates the atmospheric and spatial character of the archive.
It manages:

- the pastel sky gradient
- drifting clouds
- the translucent liminal pool
- VHS scanlines and animated grain
- translucent floating bubbles
- glass-like popup windows
- popup shadows and blur
- responsive bubble positioning
- typography and interface hierarchy

The CSS should make the environment feel layered and tactile while keeping the composition visually quiet.

### JavaScript

JavaScript transforms the environment from a static composition into a spatial archive.

It manages:

- opening and closing popup windows
- generating windows from predefined content
- dragging and repositioning windows
- managing window focus and z-index
- opening and closing transitions
- the “Enter the Pool” interaction
- randomly selected textual fragments
- changes in atmosphere associated with the pool mode

Popup content can be stored as JavaScript objects so that the archive remains structurally contained within the main environment rather than divided across separate pages.

Randomness should be used selectively to make individual encounters feel different while maintaining the overall structure of the archive.

# Design Principles

## Less Information, More Atmosphere

The archive should not explain every element. Visual space, movement, and interaction should carry part of the meaning.

## Envrionment Before Navigation

Visitors should encounter the Dreamcore landscape before being asked to understand the structure of the archive. Navigation should emerge naturally from the environment.

## Fragmented Rather Than Linear

Information should behave like fragments of memory rather than chapters in a conventional website. Visitors should be able to create their own sequence of encounters.

## Empty Space as Material

Negative space is not unused space. It creates isolation, uncertainty, and a sense that something might appear within the environment.

## Interaction as Discovery

Interactions should reveal something rather than simply operate the interface. Opening a window, moving it, or entering the pool should feel like discovering another layer of the environment.

## Controlled Uncertainty

Randomness, ambiguous text, environmental movement, and incomplete information can create uncertainty, but the system should remain coherent enough for visitors to understand how to explore it.

## Archive Cohesion

The Dreamcore environment establishes the visual and spatial language of the larger Dream Archive. Individual objects can have their own identities inside the popup windows while remaining connected through the shared atmosphere, typography, and spatial system.
